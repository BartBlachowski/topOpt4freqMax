## 0. Headline

The protocol is unusually careful. Its governance (protocol hashing, blinded acceptance
freeze, censoring discipline, "any ordering is acceptable", explicit non-equivalence of
count units) is better than most published benchmark methodology. Several of its design
choices that look suspicious are, on inspection, correct: `Q^ref` as a *window minimum*
correctly absorbs oscillation; the refusal to instantiate `A` without an external target
is honest; the `nInner=1` claim is exactly right; the extension rule is genuinely masked
and formulaic.

It nevertheless fails at two points, both verifiable now, before any run:

1. **The T1 topology gate is not satisfiable by Du–Olhoff at the fine end of the mesh
   range.** Recomputing T1 over all 1601 frozen Olhoff states at each of the nine meshes:
   at 800x100 **0.00 %** of states pass, and the longest passing run is **0**. At
   480x60/560x70/640x80 the longest run is 11/5/5, far below `P=100`. The sole cause at
   640x80 and 800x100 is T1's *aggregate* detached-area clause, which is the one clause
   in T1 that is **not** derived from filter resolution. Dropping only that clause
   restores runs of 226 and 298 at those meshes.
2. **The primary estimand's reference `Q^ref` is a function of the observation horizon,
   and the safety budgets differ between methods by a factor of 3.5** (Proposed 900,
   Yuksel 2000/stage, Olhoff 3200). A shorter horizon can only lower `Q^ref`, which
   lowers the 99 % bar, which moves `k_enter` earlier. The method with the shortest
   horizon is the method whose count is the paper's headline. This coupling is nowhere
   acknowledged.

Both are correctable. Neither correction can be made without a new scientific choice,
which is why the verdict is not "narrow corrections".

A third result deserves equal prominence because it decides how the paper may be
written: on the frozen 27-row endpoint evidence, **Du–Olhoff has the highest common
E1-raw ω₁ at every one of the nine meshes**, by 6.9–7.2 % over Proposed and 4.9–7.6 %
over Yuksel. That gap is 7× the R equivalence margin. R deliberately does not compare
across methods, and `A` is (correctly) not instantiated — so the study as designed
reports only the estimand on which that gap is invisible.

## AUDIT QUESTION 1 — Is the research question well-posed?

**Mostly yes, with one residual ambiguity that is a blocker for the headline sentence.**

"Minimum optimization work required to obtain a proper result" is decomposed cleanly:

| Concept | Where it lives | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| result maturation | `k_enter` | clean |
| persistence certification | `k_cert`, `P` | clean |
| algorithmic structure | `ITERATION_ACCOUNTING_SPEC.md`, stage/outer decomposition | clean |
| computational cost | `TIMING_SPEC.md`, secondary by construction | clean |
| output quality | achieved E1 + `Q^ref` beside every count (Table 3, F7) | clean in the tables, **not** in the headline sentence |

The separation is real and better than the norm. The ambiguity that remains:

**"Minimum" is not defined relative to a fixed observation horizon.** `Q^ref` is computed
over the whole observed trajectory (`ACCEPTANCE_GATE_SPEC.md` §4), so `k_enter` is a
functional of the budget `B0`, and `B0` is method-specific by a factor of 3.5. Two
honest analysts with different budgets obtain different `k_enter` for the same run.
"Minimum work" is therefore currently "minimum work relative to what this particular
budget revealed". See Finding **C2**.

Secondary ambiguity: "proper result" is method-relative in *quality* (R) but
method-absolute in *structure* (T1, volume) and method-specific in *validity* (Yuksel
Stage 2; Olhoff policy stage 2 + `N=2` + `gap₁₂≤1 %`). The composite is coherent but the
single word "proper" hides that one of its three components is self-referential. The
protocol should never print "iterations to a proper result" without the qualifier
"proper *for that method's own sustained quality*".

## AUDIT QUESTION 2 — Primary estimand R

Full treatment in `ESTIMAND_VALIDITY.md`. Answers to the six sub-questions:

**2.1 Can R systematically reward a method whose final design is poor?** Yes, and the
protocol says so. But the protocol understates the mechanism. R does not merely fail to
equalise quality — it has three structural preferences, none of which is quality:

- it rewards **early plateau** (a trajectory that stops improving early gets a low
  reference and therefore an early crossing);
- it rewards **oscillation** (because `Q^ref` is a window *minimum*, a noisy trajectory's
  reference sits below its own peaks, widening its acceptance band relative to its best);
- it penalises **steady late improvement** (a trajectory still improving at the horizon
  has its reference set late, so no early window can pass).

Only the second of these is a deliberate and good design decision (it is what makes R
robust for a method that never becomes stationary). The first and third are unexamined.
They matter here because the three methods sit at opposite ends of exactly this axis:
Proposed and Yuksel terminate natively on a max-density-change test (they plateau by
construction), whereas the Olhoff selected profile is documented as never becoming
stationary — `selected_profile.json` states
`simple_native_stopping_rule_identified: false`, "max density change remains at
move=0.0025 and no independent stationary condition generalizes".

**2.2 Does requiring common-evaluator endpoint quality alongside R prevent misleading
interpretation?** Partially, and only if the presentation rule is strengthened. Table 3
and F7 do carry achieved E1 and `Q^ref`. But Main **Table 1** — the table a reader will
photograph — carries counts and status only, no quality column. Given a 7 % measured
quality gap in favour of Du–Olhoff, Table 1 alone is misleading. Minimum correction:
**move achieved E1 and the E1 ratio-to-best-observed into Main Table 1**, not a
companion table. This is a presentation change with no scientific degrees of freedom.

**2.3 Is R comparable across methods when reference endpoints differ?** Only as a
*ratio-scale statement about each method separately*. `k_enter^Proposed < k_enter^Olhoff`
is a valid comparison of *maturation rate relative to each method's own attainable
quality*. It is not a comparison of efficiency in producing a given design quality, and
it must never be written as one. It is further compromised by the horizon asymmetry
(C2): the references are not even computed over comparable observation windows.

**2.4 What should R be called?** Not "optimization efficiency" and not "iteration
efficiency" — both imply work per unit of achieved outcome. **"Self-referenced maturation
work"** is the accurate name; **"maturation efficiency"** is acceptable if "self-
referenced" appears in the same sentence. "Convergence efficiency" is wrong: the protocol
correctly and explicitly refuses to make this a stationarity test
(`ACCEPTANCE_GATE_SPEC.md` §5), so calling it convergence would contradict its own design.

**2.5 What claims can R support?** "Under a common spectral evaluator and a common
structural-sanity gate, method M reached and held a state within 1 % of the best quality
it sustained on this trajectory after `k_enter` of its own method-level iterations, at
mesh N_e." Plus the same with `k_cert` for certification cost. Plus, with the quality
column adjacent, "…and the quality it thereby reached was ω₁ = X, which is Y % of the
best observed across the three methods."

**2.6 What must R explicitly NOT support?** That any method is more efficient, faster to
a *given* quality, better converging, cheaper per unit quality, or that fewer iterations
means less computation (per-iteration cost differs by 2.3–3.8× at 800x100 — see Q8).

**Is R suitable as the primary estimand?** Yes in kind, no in its current definition.
Self-relative maturation is a legitimate primary estimand for a paper about iteration
effort, and the protocol's refusal to fabricate an absolute target is correct. But R as
written is horizon-dependent (C2) and its threshold δ_R dominates its own value (M1 —
frozen Olhoff evidence shows the crossing moving 3–6× between δ_R = 1 % and 0.5 %). Fix
those two and R is suitable. The "does not enforce equal endpoint quality" limitation is
not the problem; it is disclosed and intentional.

## AUDIT QUESTION 3 — Secondary estimand A

**The conditional treatment is correct and should not be weakened.** Requiring
`Omega_req(mesh)` to come from a stated engineering requirement or an independently
certified reference, and writing `A_NOT_INSTANTIATED` otherwise, is the right call. The
repository genuinely contains no such target; I looked and found none.

**Is the optional best-observed benchmark scientifically useful?** Yes — more useful than
the protocol admits, and for a reason the protocol does not state. On the frozen
27-endpoint evidence the three methods are **not** within any plausible equivalence
margin of each other:

| mesh | Olhoff E1-raw ω₁ | Yuksel | Proposed | Olhoff lead over best other |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 160x20 | 166.745 | 157.167 | 153.675 | +6.1 % |
| 320x40 | 170.227 | 160.690 | 158.763 | +5.9 % |
| 560x70 | 171.272 | 160.343 | 160.722 | +6.6 % |
| 800x100 | 172.945 | 160.854 | 161.365 | +7.2 % |

(from `examples/Performance/final_campaign/common_evaluators.csv`, all 9 meshes show the
same ordering). A 6–7 % separation against a 1 % R margin means the best-observed
benchmark would almost certainly return `QUALITY_NOT_REACHED` for Proposed and Yuksel at
every mesh. That is a *result*, not a defect — and it is precisely why the benchmark
should be **mandatory rather than optional**, published as a censored-status column
beside R.

Making it optional creates the appearance (whether or not the intent) that the one
symmetric comparison available is the one that may be dropped. Minimum correction:
promote the best-observed benchmark from "optional, supplement" to "mandatory,
supplement, with its status column reproduced in Main Table 1". Keep every one of the
protocol's naming prohibitions — never "A", never "absolute", never "requirement".

**Could a later implementation quietly manufacture `Omega_req` from observed
trajectories?** The prohibition is stated three times and is unambiguous
(`ACCEPTANCE_GATE_SPEC.md` §4, protocol §"Absolute definition A", `IMPLEMENTATION_
REQUIREMENTS.md` §9). What is missing is a *mechanical* guard: nothing in
`IMPLEMENTATION_REQUIREMENTS.md` §6 forces the acceptance engine to reject an
`Omega_req` whose provenance hash is not in a pre-production, pre-unblinding manifest.
Minimum correction: require `Omega_req` to carry a provenance record hashed into the
protocol manifest *before* the first production run, and have the engine hard-fail
otherwise. No new scientific choice.

## AUDIT QUESTION 4 — Common spectral evaluation

Traced `analysis/three_method_parametric_study/study_evaluate_design.m` and compared its
three models against each method's native output on all 27 frozen endpoints.

**The evaluators are the three methods' own material models.** Verified to 9–11
significant digits:

| method | native ω₁ (160x20) | matching common evaluator | agreement |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Yuksel | 157.27842695834786 | **E2**-raw 157.27842695847701 | 1.1 significant-digit-limited match |
| Olhoff | 167.3355114036068 | **E3**-raw 167.33551140862403 | same |
| Proposed | (see below) | **E1**-raw | functional form identical; floors differ |

E1 is SIMP-cubic stiffness with **linear** mass and floor ratio 1e-6. That is Proposed's
interpolation (`proposed_practical_move02_tol001`: cubic stiffness, linear mass,
`stiffness_floor_ratio = 1e-9`, `material_void_density_ratio = 1e-9`) up to the floor
value. At 320x40 and finer, Proposed native and E1-raw agree to ~2×10⁻⁶ relative
(158.7627817615743 vs 158.76315935469182). E2 is Yuksel's piecewise mass law
(`g = x⁶` below 0.1, floor 1e-9). E3 is Olhoff's (`ρ` clipped at 1e-3 = Olhoff's
`rhomin`, same cutoff mass law).

So "E1 primary, E2/E3 as sensitivity" is structurally "**the Proposed method's own
material model is the arbiter; the competitors' models are sensitivity checks**".
`FAIRNESS_RISK_REGISTER.md` F01 says only that E1 "resembles" Proposed. That is an
understatement of a verifiable identity.

**Two facts materially reduce the severity, and I record them because a hostile reading
would omit them:**

1. All three evaluators agree closely on all 27 endpoints. Across the 27 rows the
   E1/E2/E3-raw spread is ≤0.43 % (largest: Proposed 160x20, E1 153.675 vs E2/E3
   154.335). The **quality ordering Olhoff > Yuksel ≈ Proposed is identical under all
   three**. Evaluator choice does not flip the endpoint ranking.
2. E1 does not uniquely rescue Proposed. Proposed's coarse-mesh weak-material local mode
   (native 109.05 vs 153.675 at 160x20; 108.78 vs 157.64 at 240x30) is suppressed by
   **all three** common evaluators, by different mechanisms (E1's 1e-6 floor; E2/E3's
   `x⁶` mass cutoff). The protocol's justification — that native ω cannot be the
   universal gate — is correct and is not an E1-specific argument.
3. E1 penalises grayness *more* than E2/E3 (it has no low-density mass cutoff), and
   Proposed has the highest grayness at every mesh (0.256 vs 0.132/0.081 at 160x20).
   On the level axis, E1 is the least favourable of the three to Proposed.

**Is E1 suitable for determining R?** For endpoint *levels*, yes — demonstrated
insensitivity. For **trajectory shape**, unknown and untested. R depends on when a
trajectory crosses 99 % of its own sustained floor, which depends on how fast the
evaluator's ω₁ responds to de-graying. E1, having no mass cutoff, keeps rising as
grayness falls, so it plausibly crosses *later* for the grayest method. The direction is
not established, and the protocol does not analyse it. This is the substantive gap.

**Is the reference endpoint evaluated consistently?** Yes — `Q^ref` and `Q(k)` both come
from the same unchanged evaluator on the same return-equivalent field. Good.

**Could the evaluator structurally favour a method?** Favour Proposed: E1 is its model,
and E1 suppresses the artifact its own solver exhibits. Favour Yuksel/Olhoff: E1 is
harsher on grayness than their own models, and their designs are crisper. Net effect on
*levels*: measured, negligible. Net effect on *R timing*: unmeasured.

**Are E2/E3 sufficient sensitivity checks?** They are the right checks, and they are
mandatory, which is correct. They are *insufficiently prominent*: relegating "the two
competitor methods' own material models" to a sensitivity table is not defensible when
the primary model is the third method's. Minimum correction: **report R under E1, E2 and
E3 as three co-equal columns of the primary table**, and label the conclusion
`MODEL_DEPENDENT` if they disagree. This costs nothing (the evaluations are already
mandatory — see the cost estimate in `PHASE2_READINESS.md`) and removes the objection
entirely.

**Does binarization create method-dependent penalties?** Yes, and the frozen evidence is
severe. Exact-count binarization of the Yuksel 400x50 endpoint drops common E1 from
159.968 (raw) to **109.440** (binary) — a 32 % collapse. Olhoff 480x60 drops 170.244 →
138.138. The protocol's division of roles (raw for spectra, binary for topology) means
these collapses do **not** contaminate the spectral gate — that division is correct and
should be preserved. But it does mean the binary field on which the *hard* topology gate
operates is capable of being structurally very different from the field being scored.
That is the real content of Q5.

**Is native ω prevented from becoming the universal acceptance quantity?** Yes,
explicitly and correctly, in three documents. Verified.

**Strongest reviewer objection to the spectral gate:** *"Your primary common evaluator is
one competitor's constitutive model, and the other two competitors' models appear only as
sensitivities. Why should I accept a quality arbiter that is Proposed's own
interpolation?"* The protocol's current answer ("E1 was preregistered before this study")
is a provenance answer to a symmetry question and will not satisfy a referee. The
answer that *does* work is the empirical one — the three models agree within 0.43 % and
preserve the ordering on all 27 endpoints — and the protocol should make that argument
explicitly, with the table, and then report R under all three anyway.

## AUDIT QUESTION 5 — Topology sanity gate

Full treatment in `TOPOLOGY_GATE_AUDIT.md`. Summary:

**Where `a_res = 5` comes from.** Verified arithmetically: integer cells with
`p² + q² < 1.3²` are {(0,0), (±1,0), (0,±1)} = 5. And `r = 1.3` is verified as Olhoff's
filter radius (`res.cfg.rminEl = 1.3` in every frozen `.mat`; `rmin_element: 1.3` in
`profile_freeze_manifest.json`).

**Is it a common physical/numerical scale, or another arbitrary threshold?** Neither
cleanly. The three methods use **different** filter radii in element widths — Olhoff
1.3, **Proposed 2.0, Yuksel 2.5** (all from the freeze manifest). Their footprints are 5,
**9** and **21** cells. Calling 1.3 `r_common` is a misnomer: the manifest's `"common"`
block declares that radii are expressed in element widths at every resolution, not that
the radius itself is common. Choosing the *smallest* of three yields the *strictest*
allowance, which is the conservative direction, and that is a defensible choice — but it
must be named "the strictest of the three frozen footprints", not "the common footprint".

**Does five elements mean the same physical feature across nine meshes?** No. The radius
is fixed in element widths, so the footprint is mesh-invariant in *resolution* units —
which is the sense the protocol claims — but its physical area shrinks 25× from 160x20 to
800x100, and its share of the solid phase falls from 4/1600 = 0.25 % to 4/40000 = 0.01 %.
T1 is therefore 25× stricter, in relative terms, at the mesh that determines the top of
every scaling fit.

**Empirical feasibility — the decisive result.** I recomputed the exact-count
volume-preserving projection, four-connected components, and support-footprint
connectivity for **every one of the 1601 frozen Olhoff states at each of the nine
meshes** (14 409 states; read-only, from `examples/Performance/final_campaign/raw/olhoff/`):

| mesh | states | T1 pass % | longest T1 run | ≥ P=100 ? | T1a pass % | longest T1a run | ≥100 ? | T0 longest run |
|---|---:|---:|---:|:--:|---:|---:|:--:|---:|
| 160x20 | 1601 | 66.31 | 967 | yes | 74.62 | 1065 | yes | 944 |
| 240x30 | 1601 | 77.75 | 1132 | yes | 88.19 | 1265 | yes | 1122 |
| 320x40 | 1601 | 56.50 | 861 | yes | 60.12 | 890 | yes | 181 |
| 400x50 | 1601 | 37.44 | 347 | yes | 62.00 | 526 | yes | 25 |
| 480x60 | 358 | 4.48 | **11** | **no** | 26.61 | **50** | **no** | 8 |
| 560x70 | 400 | 1.25 | **5** | **no** | 6.02 | **14** | **no** | 5 |
| 640x80 | 1067 | 0.56 | **5** | **no** | 45.78 | 226 | yes | 1 |
| 720x90 | 1601 | 16.50 | 261 | yes | 55.94 | 415 | yes | 122 |
| 800x100 | 1601 | **0.00** | **0** | **no** | 27.38 | 298 | yes | 0 |

T1 = `C_required ∧ max(detached) < 5 ∧ Σ(detached) < 5` (the specified baseline).
T1a = `C_required ∧ max(detached) < 5` (per-component clause only).
Support connectivity `C_required` passes at 95.8–99.1 % of states everywhere; it is not
the binding constraint anywhere.

Three conclusions follow, all verifiable:

1. **T1 as specified is unsatisfiable for Du–Olhoff at 800x100** — not one state in a
   full 1600-iteration trajectory passes. Olhoff would be `INVALID_TOPOLOGY` at the
   largest mesh, and at 480x60/560x70/640x80 as well (the first two also fail for solver
   reasons, but 640x80 fails on topology alone).
2. **The aggregate clause is the sole cause at 640x80 and 800x100.** At those meshes the
   largest detached component is 4 elements (per-component rule passes) while the
   detached *total* is 20 and 32. Removing only the aggregate clause restores runs of
   226 and 298.
3. **The aggregate clause is the one part of T1 with no derivation.** "No detached
   component may exceed one filter footprint" is a genuine resolution argument: a feature
   smaller than the filter footprint is below the method's own resolvable scale.
   "*All* detached material together must be below one footprint" is a different and much
   stronger claim, and nothing about filter resolution implies it. As the mesh refines,
   the number of sub-resolution specks grows with element count while the aggregate cap
   stays at 4 elements — so the clause is actively hostile to the mesh-scaling study it
   is embedded in.

**Does the filter footprint differ among methods?** Yes (5 / 9 / 21 cells). **Whose
filter defines `a_res`?** Olhoff's — the strictest. **Can the rule favour a method that
fragments less?** Yes, and the available evidence says it does: raw-threshold LCC at the
frozen endpoints is 1.000 for Proposed at **all nine** meshes, while Olhoff is < 1 at six
and Yuksel at two. T1 differentially censors the two competitors and not Proposed.

**Is hard support connectivity correctly defined?** Yes. The benchmark is an 8×1 beam with
both translations fixed at the mid-height node of each end face — verified identically in
`study_evaluate_design.m` (`jMid=round(nely/2)`), `topopt_freq.m`
(`j_mid=floor(nely/2)`), `top99neo_inertial_freq.m` (`midRow=round(nely/2)+1`) and
`Matlab/reproduction2007/fem/model2D.m` (`support='mid'`, which errors on odd `nely`).
`floor` and `round` coincide because every one of the nine meshes has even `nely`; that
should be recorded as a precondition. Requiring one four-connected component to intersect
both support element footprints is the right rule, and it is a genuine improvement on the
evaluator's existing column-based `left_right_connected`.

**Should load-path connectivity also be required?** No. There is no common external load
point across the three formulations (Proposed uses a frozen-reference semi-harmonic load,
Yuksel Stage 1 a point load, Olhoff none), so any load-path rule would be method-specific
and would violate the protocol's own symmetry principle. The protocol's reasoning here is
correct.

**Is exact-count volume-preserving binarization method-neutral?** In its intent, yes — it
is the right way to stop one method's grayness from admitting more material to the
structural graph, and the stable index tie-break is deterministic. In its consequences,
no: it is precisely the operation that turned Yuksel 400x50 from ω₁ = 159.97 into 109.44.
That is a real property of that design, not an artifact of the rule, and exposing it is
the point. Neutral procedure, non-neutral consequences — which is acceptable provided
the raw/binary pair is always shown, as the protocol already requires.

**Verdict on T1 as baseline:** **not suitable as written; suitable after one deletion.**
Drop the aggregate clause and T1 becomes `C_required ∧ n_islands_resolved == 0` — a
single, fully derived, legible rule that the topology spec already computes and names.
Retain `Σ(detached)`, `f_LCC`, and the component-area distribution as mandatory reported
diagnostics. Keep T0 as the strict sensitivity, but see the next point.

**T0 as "sensitivity" is pre-decided.** The recomputation shows T0 would censor Olhoff at
400x50, 480x60, 560x70, 640x80 and 800x100 — five of nine. The protocol's rule ("if T0
changes a substantive ordering, call the conclusion `TOPOLOGY_RESOLUTION_SENSITIVE`")
therefore has a known answer before the study runs. A sensitivity whose outcome is
already determined by frozen evidence is not a sensitivity. Either state that outcome up
front as a known property, or replace T0 with a sensitivity that can actually discriminate.

## AUDIT QUESTION 6 — `k_enter` vs `k_cert`

**6.1 Which best answers "minimum iterations to obtain a proper result"?** `k_enter`. It
is the location of the state that first satisfied every acceptance condition and went on
satisfying them. That is what "obtain" means.

**6.2 Which best answers "minimum iterations required to certify a proper result"?**
`k_cert`. Unambiguously.

**6.3 Is presenting both sufficient to avoid ambiguity?** Yes for a careful reader; no for
the abstract. The protocol requires equal visual prominence in tables and figures, which
is right. It does not constrain the abstract or the caption, which is where a single
number will be quoted. Minimum correction: add a binding rule that any single-number
statement of iteration effort must name which endpoint it is and must not appear without
its quality context.

**6.4 Should either be the headline?** `k_enter` should lead, as the protocol says,
**provided** it is always named a retrospective quantity. `k_cert` cannot be the headline
because it is dominated by an arbitrary convention (`P`) — at `P=100` it is
`k_enter + 99`, and for Proposed at 160x20 that constant is comparable to the method's
entire native run of 107 iterations.

**6.5 Is there look-ahead bias in `k_enter`?** Yes, structurally and unavoidably —
twice over. First, `k_enter` is only knowable after observing `P` further states.
Second and more seriously, `k_enter` depends on `Q^ref`, which depends on the *entire*
observed trajectory including everything after `k_cert`. So `k_enter` uses information
from the far future of the run, not merely `P` states ahead.

**6.6 Is that bias problematic given the retrospective framing?** The first form, no —
"retrospective maturation location" is an honest and standard framing, and `k_cert` is
supplied as the prospective counterpart. The second form, **yes, and it is not disclosed**.
"Retrospective within a `P`-window" and "dependent on the entire budget" are different
claims. Combined with method-specific budgets differing by 3.5×, the second form becomes
Finding **C2**: `k_enter` is not merely retrospective, it is *budget-relative*, and the
budgets are not comparable. The protocol's framing covers the first and is silent on the
second.

## AUDIT QUESTION 7 — Persistence `P = 100`

Full treatment in `PERSISTENCE_AUDIT.md`. Summary of the aggressive reading:

**Is `P=100` justified by Olhoff evidence, or generalised from it?** Generalised from it,
and the provenance is explicit enough to trace. The Olhoff stabilization policy hard-codes
`persistence == 100` (`olhoffOptStabilized.m` asserts
`p.persistence==100 && p.gap_threshold==0.01`, "Policy differs from preregistration"),
and the prior audits found 50-step look-ahead false positives *on Olhoff trajectories*.
That is a sound basis for `P=100` **for Olhoff**. It is not evidence about Proposed or
Yuksel, whose native runs are 107–330 and 244–2000 total iterations respectively, and
whose stopping rule is a max-density-change test that guarantees a plateau.

**Effect on the comparison, quantified from frozen counts:**

| method | prior native count range | `P-1 = 99` as a share of that run |
|---|---|---|
| Proposed | 107 – 330 OC updates | **30 % – 93 %** |
| Yuksel | 244 – 2000 total (S1+S2) | 5 % – 41 % |
| Olhoff | 1600 fixed outer | **6 %** |

So the fixed additive burden is between 5× and 15× larger, *in proportion to the work
being measured*, for Proposed than for Olhoff. The protocol's F10 states the direction
correctly ("Adds a large fixed burden to short Proposed trajectories"). It does not state
the magnitude, and the magnitude is what matters: for Proposed at 160x20, certification
would require running roughly as many extra iterations as the method's entire native run.

**Consequences, each checked:**

- *Compresses relative differences* — yes, mechanically, and most for the shortest series.
- *Changes method ranking* — no, it cannot: `k_cert` is `k_enter + 99` for every method,
  so rank order in absolute counts is preserved exactly. Ratios change; order does not.
  The protocol states this correctly.
- *Distorts scaling exponents* — yes, and I can quantify it on real data. Using the four
  frozen Olhoff `persistent_raw_E1_1pct` values [186, 234, 234, 246] at
  N_e ∈ {3200, 7200, 12800, 20000}: free power fit gives `p = +0.145`; adding 99 to every
  point gives `p = +0.099`. **A 32 % reduction in the fitted exponent from a bookkeeping
  convention.**
- *Disproportionately penalises expensive Olhoff outer iterations* — in **time**, yes:
  99 extra outer iterations at 800x100 cost 99 × 1.844 s ≈ 183 s versus 99 × 0.814 s ≈ 81 s
  for Proposed. In **iterations**, no. The protocol shows both, which is the right answer.
- *Interacts unfairly with Yuksel stage boundaries* — guarded correctly: only Stage 2 is
  acceptance-eligible, so the window cannot straddle the handoff. But note the window can
  force Stage 2 past its native stop, and Stage 1's own budget change (see M6) shifts the
  Stage-2 starting point.
- *Interacts with Proposed's native stopping* — yes, materially. At 160x20 Proposed's
  native run is 107 iterations and `k_cert ≥ 100` by construction, so `k_cert` will very
  likely exceed the native run length. The observer-extension machinery handles this
  mechanically (bitwise prefix identity is already validated in
  `extension_invariance_validation.json`), but the *semantics* are odd: the reported
  "minimum optimization work" for Proposed can exceed the work Proposed performs when
  actually used.
- *Unnecessarily conservative* — for Proposed and Yuksel, probably yes; for Olhoff, no.

**Same `P` for all methods, or method-specific?** Same `P` is correct and should be kept.
The protocol's reasoning is right: `P` is an *evidence burden*, and varying it by method
means requiring different amounts of proof for the same claim. Method-specific `P` would
create exactly the degree of freedom that the firewall exists to prevent — a knob that
could be tuned per method after seeing counts. Do not adopt it.

**Is `P=100` defensible?** As a *frozen, disclosed convention* whose effects are reported
separately: yes. As a *scientifically derived* value: no — it is Olhoff-derived and
Olhoff-appropriate, applied to methods for which no such evidence exists.

**What principle should determine `P`?** If `P` is ever re-derived, the principle should
be: *`P` is the smallest window length for which the false-certification rate, measured on
the pre-existing frozen trajectories of all three methods by counting windows that pass
and then exit, is below a preregistered rate.* That is measurable now, from existing data,
before production, without new optimization — the Olhoff trajectories alone give 14 409
states, and the `crossings()`-style exit counts in the stabilization audit are the right
statistic. Deriving `P` this way would make it evidence-based for all three methods rather
than one. **Do not** derive `P` from desired iteration counts; and if `P=100` is retained
unchanged, that is acceptable provided it is labelled a convention inherited from the
Olhoff stabilization policy, not a property of the three methods.

## AUDIT QUESTION 8 — Iteration accounting

Full treatment in `ITERATION_ACCOUNTING_AUDIT.md`.

### Proposed — verified

One counted iteration = one execution of the single loop in
`analysis/ourApproach/Matlab/topopt_freq.m` (lines 412–678): assemble `M(x)`, assemble
`K(x)`, one FE solve for all load cases, filter sensitivities, one OC update, re-filter to
`xPhys`, compute `change = max|x − x_old|` on the **raw design field**. The loop counter
`loop` is the count and `nIter = loop`. The post-loop eigensolve (line ~718) is outside the
loop and is correctly excluded. `xOut = xPhys(:)` (line 762), so the return-equivalent
state at `k` is the post-update filtered field — matching the protocol's convention.
**One counted iteration is one meaningful method-level OC update. Claim verified.**

One structural fact the accounting spec should state and does not: with
`semi_harmonic_baseline = solid` and `reference_refresh_interval = 0`, the reference
eigenpair is computed **once before the loop and never refreshed** (line 441:
`dueFlags(k) = (loop == 1) && ~strcmp(harmonicBaseline,'solid')` is false throughout).
So a Proposed iteration contains **no eigensolve**, whereas an Olhoff outer iteration
contains one and the eigensolve is 75 % of its cost at 800x100 (median `tEig` 1.368 s of
1.817 s total). This is the single largest source of per-iteration non-equivalence and
belongs in the main-table footnote, stated numerically.

### Yuksel — one factual error in the spec

`N_stage1`, `N_stage2`, `N_total = N_stage1 + N_stage2` are correctly defined as loop
passes of `localComplianceLoop` and `localInertialLoop`.

**The spec's stated justification is wrong.** `ITERATION_ACCOUNTING_SPEC.md` asserts:
"In the current MATLAB implementation its locally updated design variable is not returned
into Stage 2". `analysis/YukselApproach/Matlab/top99neo_inertial_freq.m` line 237 reads
`x = xPhys;` under the comment "Use stage-1 outputs as initial guesses for stage 2", and
line 242 `[xPhys_stage2,U_stage2] = deal(xPhys,U);`. **Stage 1's physical field becomes
Stage 2's design variable, and Stage 1's displacement becomes Stage 2's mode estimate.**
The design state is carried forward; only the raw/physical identification is
discontinuous (Stage 1's *filtered* field is re-used as Stage 2's *unfiltered* variable,
a one-time re-filtering shift).

Consequences: (a) the erroneous sentence must be corrected — a referee who opens the file
will find it in under a minute; (b) the correction *strengthens* the case for summing,
because the density trajectory really is continuous across the handoff; (c) the real
justification for excluding Stage 1 from acceptance is the one the protocol also gives —
Stage 1 minimises compliance under a point load, not the eigenfrequency objective — and
that justification survives intact.

**Is summing scientifically meaningful?** Yes, as *chronological work required before a
Stage-2 result can be returned*, which is exactly how the spec phrases it. The two
iteration types are not equal work — Stage 1 solves `K(x)U = F_point`, Stage 2 additionally
assembles `M(x)` and forms a design-dependent inertial load each pass — but they are
sequential and both are mandatory. **Main table phrasing:** `N_total` must never be
column-headed "iterations". Head it **"chronological method-level updates (S1 + S2)"**
with `N_stage1` and `N_stage2` shown adjacent, exactly as `PROPOSED_TABLE_LAYOUTS.md`
Table 1 already does. That layout is correct as drawn.

### Olhoff — verified, with one naming problem

- **outer iteration**: one pass of the `for outer = 1:cfg.maxOuter` loop in
  `olhoffOptStabilized.m` — one generalized eigensolve + multiplicity detection, one
  generalized-gradient construction + filtering, one LP call, one accepted update.
  Verified.
- **LP call / `nInner`**: `Matlab/reproduction2007/algo/innerLoopLP.m` makes exactly one
  `linprog(...,'dual-simplex-highs')` call and returns `st = struct('nInner',1,...)`
  hard-coded. Confirmed in the frozen data: `hist.nInner` is uniformly 1 across all 1600
  entries at 160x20 (`unique = [1]`, `sum = 1600`). **The protocol's claim that `nInner=1`
  is one `linprog` call and must never be reported as a HiGHS/simplex iteration is exactly
  correct.**
- **solver-internal iteration**: the distinction is proven by the frozen 640x80 failure
  replay — `linprog_exitflag = 0`, `lp_iterations = 38`, message "Solver stopped
  prematurely" (`olhoff_640_failure_diagnostics.csv`). Verified.

**Are outer iterations the correct method-level unit?** Yes. It is the level at which the
design is updated and at which the method's own literature counts.

**Does LP-call count add information?** Essentially none while the policy is one call per
successful update — `sum(nInner) == N_outer` exactly. The protocol's recommendation
(footnote, not a main column, promoted only if a retry policy ever makes them differ) is
correct.

**Are solver-internal LP iterations meaningful work metrics?** No — they are
backend-dependent diagnostics of MATLAB R2025b's `dual-simplex-highs` on a degenerate LP,
not a property of the Du–Olhoff formulation. Supplementary placement is correct. One
caveat for claim boundaries: capturing them requires editing `innerLoopLP.m` (adding the
4th `linprog` output), which is a file in the frozen reproduction algorithm tree guarded
by `repro2007_tree_hash.m`. Behaviourally inert, but it crosses the harness/algorithm
boundary the protocol itself draws — see Q18.

### Cross-method — can they share one plot?

**Yes, with a qualification that must be on the figure, not in the caption.** The plot
measures *method-level design-update count to the accepted endpoint*. That is a
well-defined, honest quantity for all three. What it does not measure is computational
work, and the disparity is large and now quantified: at 800x100 the frozen campaign gives
1.844 s per Olhoff outer iteration, 0.814 s per Proposed OC update and 0.489 s per Yuksel
iteration — a **2.3× and 3.8× spread**, with the Olhoff eigensolve alone accounting for
75 % of its cost.

Required qualification: the F1 panels must carry, in-axes, a note of the form
"method-level update counts; per-update cost differs by up to 3.8× at N_e = 80 000 — see
F2", and F2 must be adjacent, not merely referenced. The protocol places F2 in the
mandatory main set, which is right; it does not require the in-axes cross-reference, which
it should. Demanding false equivalence would be worse than the current design — the
protocol is correct to refuse it.

## AUDIT QUESTION 9 — Timing

Full treatment in `TIMING_AND_SCALING_AUDIT.md`. Verdict: **the timing design is fair and
is the strongest part of the package.**

Checked, and present: warm-up policy (one discarded warm-up per method, outside the
production mesh set); thread pinning (`maxNumCompThreads(1)`, single-thread, serial,
recorded); solver startup and version capture (MATLAB update, `linprog`/HiGHS version and
exact options, eigensolver settings, start-vector policy); initialization
(`T_init`, explicitly including Proposed's frozen-solid reference eigensolve — the right
call, since it is real work Proposed must do); terminal eigensolve (excluded from
iterations, timed separately as `T_native_finalize` — correct, and symmetric because
Olhoff also gets a `T_native_finalize`); setup time; per-stage timing (Yuksel stages kept
separate and a combined mean permitted only as an explicitly labelled time/count
summary); post-processing (disclosed, not charged); repeated-run policy (median of three
serial replays, range and MAD, frozen balanced randomised order, and — importantly — a
failed or mismatched replay is investigated and visibly excluded rather than dropped as
an outlier); deterministic replay (trajectory fingerprints verified at both endpoints,
supported by the already-passing bitwise prefix identity in
`extension_invariance_validation.json`).

`T_enter`, `T_cert` and mean iteration time are reproducibly defined. The separation of
observer/I/O-contaminated discovery runs from clean fixed-horizon replays is exactly right
and is what most published comparisons get wrong.

**Is any component unfairly excluded for one method?** I looked specifically for this and
found one asymmetry, which turns out to be immaterial: Olhoff's `T_init`/`T_native_finalize`
are unattributed in the frozen runner and the spec records them as `NA` rather than zero
(correct policy). Magnitude check from `table1_performance.csv` at 800x100: Olhoff's
unattributed overhead is `total_wall − loop = 2953.97 − 2950.50 = 3.47 s` against a
2950 s loop; Proposed's init + post is `1.67 + 2.69 = 4.36 s` against a 269 s loop. So the
unattributed Olhoff time is of the same absolute size as Proposed's attributed time and
is negligible against both loops. The `NA` is a reporting gap, not a fairness gap. I record
this because a hostile reviewer would flag the `NA` and the honest answer is that it does
not matter here — but it should be measured in Phase 2 anyway, since it is cheap.

Two residual points:
- The fallback clause ("if three large replays are rejected, report instrumented kernel
  timers labelled `single-run descriptive`, and do not mix policies across methods") is
  correct but should also forbid mixing across *meshes*, which it currently does not say.
- Common-evaluator time is excluded equally for all methods, which is right, and the
  offline gate cost is now estimated (≈6 h for E1-only across all 27 cells; see
  `PHASE2_READINESS.md`), so the exclusion is not hiding a large number.

## AUDIT QUESTION 10 — Scaling curves

Full treatment in `TIMING_AND_SCALING_AUDIT.md`.

**Nine resolutions — sufficient?** For a *descriptive* fit, yes. For an exponent anyone
should quote, marginal. The nine meshes span N_e ∈ [3200, 80 000] = **1.398 decades**, and
they are linearly spaced in `nelx`, so in log N_e the spacing is
[0.352, 0.250, 0.194, 0.158, 0.134, 0.116, 0.102, 0.092] — **five of the nine points lie in
the last half-decade**. The smallest mesh carries by far the most leverage. Jackknifing the
frozen four-mesh Olhoff series: dropping N_e = 3200 moves `p` from **+0.145 to +0.047**
(a 3× change); dropping any other point moves it to +0.148…+0.173.

**Censored and failed cases** are handled well: only certified baseline-R cells are
fit-eligible, `PASS_WITH_LATER_FAILURE` stays eligible (correct — certification preceded
the failure), everything else is plotted with a distinct hollow/lower-bound marker, never
connected, never regressed. Explicit prohibitions on imputation, pooling, outlier removal
and post-hoc subrange selection are all present. This is good practice.

**The gap the spec leaves open — comparing exponents fitted on different supports.** The
spec requires reporting `Ne_min`/`Ne_max` and forbids extrapolation, but it does not
forbid placing `p_Proposed` next to `p_Olhoff` in one table when they were fitted on
different mesh subsets. The completed campaign already does exactly this:
`table1_complexity_fit.csv` reports Olhoff `p=1.193` on **6** points, Yuksel `p=1.706` on
**7**, Proposed `p=1.418` on **9**. Those three exponents are not comparable — Olhoff's
excludes 480/560/640, Yuksel's excludes the two largest meshes. Given the T1 result (Q5),
the new study will have *worse* support asymmetry. Minimum correction: require a companion
**common-support fit** restricted to the meshes valid for all three methods, reported
beside the per-method full-range fit, and forbid any cross-method exponent comparison
outside common support.

**`R²_log`** is correctly computed on log values and correctly labelled. **`n_valid`** and
minimum-three-point rules are present and adequate. **Fitting range** is bounded to observed
data. **Log-log OLS** is the right estimator for a power law with multiplicative error.
**Weighting**: none, which is the correct default for unit-weight log-space OLS, though it
means the high-leverage smallest mesh dominates — the jackknife above should be reported
for that reason. **Heteroscedasticity** is not addressed and cannot be, with one
deterministic run per cell; the spec's refusal to draw confidence bands is the honest
response, but a bare `p` with no interval invites over-reading. Minimum correction: report
a **leave-one-out range of `p`** alongside `p` — deterministic, assumes no sampling model,
and directly communicates the leverage problem. **`C`** is correctly stated to retain the
plotted quantity's units; it should additionally be flagged as an extrapolation to
N_e = 1, which is meaningless here, so `C` should never be interpreted physically.

**Empirical scaling vs asymptotic complexity.** The spec already prohibits "asymptotic
complexity", "order-optimal" and equivalents, requires captions to say "empirical scaling
over the tested mesh range", and retires the old fixed-`p=1.5` plot. That is exactly the
right call and I endorse it without reservation. With 1.4 decades, one run per cell, and
method-dependent censoring, **only empirical scaling is supportable.** Any complexity claim
would additionally need an operation-count model, which this study does not have.

One further hazard specific to this study: Proposed's frozen count series is
**non-monotone** in N_e — 107, 236, 207, 182, 219, 256, 309, 297, 330 — with only ~3×
dynamic range over a 25× range in N_e. A power law fitted to that will have a small,
poorly identified exponent and a modest `R²_log`. That is a legitimate finding if it
occurs, but it must be reported as "weakly identified", not as a small exponent.

## AUDIT QUESTION 11 — Certification burden and scaling

`k_cert(N_e) = k_enter(N_e) + 99` at baseline `P`. Fitting `C·N_e^p` to that is fitting a
power law to a function that **is not a power law** — it is a power law plus a constant.
The fitted exponent is therefore a property of the sum, and it varies with the size of the
counts relative to 99.

Quantified on frozen Olhoff data (four meshes, `persistent_raw_E1_1pct`):

| series | C | p | R²_log |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `k_enter` | 59.91 | **+0.1451** | 0.839 |
| `k_enter + 99` | 131.5 | **+0.0991** | 0.843 |

A 32 % reduction in the exponent from the certification convention alone. The distortion
is largest where counts are smallest — i.e. for Proposed, whose counts are 107–330.

**Recommendation (not applied):** make **`k_enter` scaling primary** and **`k_cert` scaling
secondary/descriptive**, and state in the `k_cert` fit caption that the exponent is
convention-dependent and reported for completeness. Better still, also report the fit of
`k_cert − (P−1)` as an identity check; it must reproduce `p_enter` exactly, which makes the
convention effect visible in one line.

The protocol already says most of this — it requires independent fits, warns about
flattening, and mandates the `P=50/200` sensitivity. What it does **not** do is
subordinate `k_cert` scaling to `k_enter` scaling; it treats both as co-equal mandatory
layers (protocol §"Mandatory efficiency and scaling story", layers 1 and 2). Given the
quantified 32 % distortion, co-equal status is too generous to a quantity whose exponent
is partly an artifact of `P`.

## AUDIT QUESTION 12 — Safety budget policy

**The extension rule is well constructed.** One tranche of `0.5·B0`; four simultaneous
conditions (solver health, volume + connectivity throughout the last block, sustained E1
floor improved from the penultimate `P` block to the last, and not already certified);
masked method/mesh labels; only those fields visible to the reviewer; every decision
published; `1.5·B0` without a gate produces a final `NOT_REACHED`; any further extension
requires an amendment that may not change acceptance or algorithm settings. The clean-rerun
requirement (extension must be a fresh run from the same initialization with the whole
retained prefix matching, else classify an implementation failure) is a genuinely strong
control and is already backed by passing bitwise prefix-identity evidence.

**Does it prevent repeated extension until a desired result appears?** Yes. The tranche
count is one, the trigger is formulaic and computable without seeing identities, and the
"already certified" condition stops extension being used to improve an existing pass.

**Can extension occur without changing algorithm parameters / tolerances / move limits /
filters / gates / persistence / evaluators?** Yes — the mechanism is the existing
observer-extension mode that suppresses only native termination, and
`extension_invariance_validation.json` already demonstrates bitwise prefix identity for
all three methods at 160x20 (`max_abs_diff_xphys_at_stop = 0`, `scalar_prefix_identical:
true`, `bitwise: true`). The protocol correctly extends this to one coarse and one fine
mesh per method.

**Blind or deterministic?** It is both, which is the right answer: deterministic
preregistered rule, evaluated on masked labels. Nothing to correct.

**Does `NOT_REACHED` remain genuinely possible?** Yes — and on the frozen evidence it is
not merely possible but *likely*, which is the strongest sign the policy is real. Olhoff
will hit `GENUINE_SOLVER_FAILURE` at three meshes (the LP failures reproduce
deterministically), and under T1 as written it would additionally be `INVALID_TOPOLOGY`
at 800x100 with zero passing states.

**The one defect, and it is not in the extension rule but in what the budget does to the
estimand.** `B0` is 900 for Proposed, 2000 per stage for Yuksel and 3200 for Olhoff.
`Q^ref` is a maximum over windows within the observed horizon, so a longer horizon can
only raise `Q^ref`, which raises the 99 % bar, which delays `k_enter`. The method with the
shortest budget therefore gets the most easily satisfied self-reference. The formula
`B0 = ceil_100(max(2·K_prior, K_prior + 5P))` was derived from prior work without
reference to future ordering — that part is clean — but its *consequence* for `Q^ref`
comparability was never examined. This is Finding **C2**.

## AUDIT QUESTION 13 — Expected-result firewall

**Verdict: the firewall holds in governance and in stated intent, and fails in the joint
direction of its unexamined choices.**

What genuinely holds. The profiles were frozen before this study, on a preregistered
Pareto rule, with holdout and forbidden mesh sets recorded
(`profile_freeze_manifest.json`, `frozen_before_any_holdout_mesh_was_run: true`,
`retuning_after_this_point: PROHIBITED`). The risk register enumerates the objections
against all three methods rather than against an expected Proposed advantage, and several
entries name ways the design could hurt Proposed (F01 E1 grayness penalty, F06 gray
reinterpretation, F07 T0 tie-pixel veto, F10 the `P` burden). The protocol states that any
ordering is acceptable and names four specific surprising orderings as valid outcomes.
The old campaign is explicitly a firewall, not a data source. `A` is refused rather than
fabricated. These are not the actions of a design aimed at a conclusion.

What does not hold. Six choices were each made for a locally stated reason, and all six
point the same way. None was analysed for direction.

| choice | stated reason | direction |
|---|---|---|
| R self-relative, primary | absolute target would be fabricated | removes the axis where Olhoff leads by 6–7 % at all nine meshes |
| `A` not instantiated; best-observed benchmark *optional* | no independent target exists | the one symmetric quality comparison is the one that may be omitted |
| E1 primary, E2/E3 sensitivity | E1 preregistered first | the primary arbiter is Proposed's own interpolation; the competitors' models are demoted |
| T1 aggregate clause | "measurement-resolution allowance" | undefended clause; verified to censor Olhoff at 4 meshes incl. 800x100 (0 % pass), and Proposed's raw LCC is 1.000 at all nine |
| method-specific gates | the formulations differ | Yuksel and Olhoff each carry a count-raising gate; Proposed carries none. Olhoff's policy-stage-2 trigger fires at iteration **245** at 160x20, so Olhoff's `k_enter` there cannot be below ~245 regardless of design quality |
| `B0` = 900 / 2000 / 3200 | derived from prior work | shortest horizon → lowest `Q^ref` → earliest `k_enter`, for the method whose count is the headline |

Each is individually defensible. I could construct an innocent explanation for every one,
and the documents supply those explanations. But a firewall is not a set of individually
defensible choices; it is a demonstration that the choices are not *jointly* directional.
Six-for-six in the same direction, with no directional analysis anywhere in the package,
is not a firewall — it is an untested assumption that the choices are independent.

**Minimum correction, and it is cheap:** add a **directional-bias register** — for each
frozen choice, state which method it is expected to favour and by what mechanism, and
record whether the direction was checked against existing evidence. Two of the six can be
checked *now*, before production, from frozen data (T1 feasibility — done in this audit;
evaluator level-effect — done in this audit, and it is small). The remaining four cannot
be checked without the new runs, and should be declared as open directional risks in the
paper rather than dispersed into a 27-row risk table where their common direction is
invisible.

I want to be explicit about what I am and am not saying. I am not saying the protocol was
engineered toward a conclusion. The governance is too good and the self-criticism too
specific for that reading. I am saying that the package cannot currently demonstrate that
it wasn't, and that two of its choices (T1, `B0`→`Q^ref`) are wrong on the merits in ways
that happen to point the same way as the other four.

## AUDIT QUESTION 14 — The exact acceptance expression

The protocol's documents do define this unambiguously once assembled. Reconstructed from
`ACCEPTANCE_GATE_SPEC.md` §§1–5 and `TOPOLOGY_SANITY_SPEC.md`:

```
X_mj(k)  :=  return-equivalent physical density field after exactly k completed
             method-level iterations of method m on mesh j

# --- pointwise, non-spectral ---
H_health(k) := every required solve and update through k finite and flag-successful
H_V(k)      := | mean(X(k)) - Vf | / Vf  <=  1e-3                       # raw field
xb(k)       := exact-count volume-preserving binary projection of X(k)
                 nSolid = round(Vf * Ne), sort by decreasing density,
                 stable tie-break on global element index
H_T(k)      := C_required(xb(k)) == 1                                   # support-footprint,
               AND max_area(detached components of xb(k))      <  a_res  #   4-connected
               AND sum_area(detached components of xb(k))      <  a_res  # a_res = 5
H_spec(k)   := Proposed : TRUE
               Yuksel   : state k lies in Stage 2
               Olhoff   : policyStage(k) == 2
                          AND N(k) == 2
                          AND gap12(k) = (w2-w1)/w1 <= 0.01              # native model,
                                                                        # same return-equiv state
H_m(k)      := H_health(k) AND H_V(k) AND H_T(k) AND H_spec(k)

# --- spectral, primary estimand R ---
Q(k)        := omega1 from unchanged common E1 evaluator on raw X(k)
W_mj        := { all length-P windows W within the final observer horizon
                 such that H_m(k) == 1 for every k in W }
Q_ref_mj    := max over W in W_mj of ( min over k in W of Q(k) )         # best sustained floor
S_R(k)      := Q(k) >= (1 - delta_R) * Q_ref_mj                          # delta_R = 0.01

# --- composite ---
A_R(k)      := H_m(k) AND S_R(k)
k_enter     := min { a >= 1 : A_R(k) == 1 for all k in [a, a+P-1] }      # P = 100
k_cert      := k_enter + P - 1
```

with `NOT_REACHED` and its sub-classes assigned by the §6 precedence when no such `a`
exists, and `REFERENCE_INVALID` when `W_mj` is empty.

**Two things this expression makes visible that prose obscures:**

1. `Q_ref_mj` is quantified over "the final observer horizon" — so `A_R(k)`, and therefore
   `k_enter`, is a functional of `B0`. The expression cannot be evaluated without naming
   the budget. This is Finding **C2** in one line.
2. `H_T` contains two independent area clauses sharing one constant. Written out, it is
   obvious that the second clause does not follow from the resolution argument that
   justifies the first. This is Finding **C1** in one line.

**Is this a blocker?** The expression itself is not a blocker — it is complete,
unambiguous, and mechanically implementable. But it is currently distributed across two
documents and four sections, and neither document writes it out. Minimum correction:
publish this expression verbatim in the protocol as the normative definition, so that the
two defects above are visible at the point of freezing rather than after production.

The four-way separation holds for almost everything.

**The one item.** Capturing `linprog`'s solver-internal iteration count requires changing

```matlab
[x, ~, flag] = linprog(f, A, b, Aeq, beq, lb, ub, opts);
```

in `Matlab/reproduction2007/algo/innerLoopLP.m` to also take the `output` struct. The call
is behaviourally identical — same matrices, bounds, objective, algorithm, ordering, and
returned point — so this is observational instrumentation by any reasonable definition, and
`IMPLEMENTATION_REQUIREMENTS.md` §5 already requires a mirror-identity test for exactly
this. But the file sits inside the frozen Du–Olhoff reproduction algorithm tree, which is
hash-guarded by `repro2007_tree_hash.m`, and editing it will break that hash. That is a
governance collision, not a scientific one, and the protocol does not mention it.

Minimum correction: either (a) instrument via a diagnostic mirror outside the frozen tree —
the pattern already used by
`analysis/performance_campaign_targeted_replays/innerLoopLPDiagnostic.m` and
`olhoffOptStabilizedDiagnostic.m`, which is clearly the intended path — and state that
explicitly; or (b) accept the hash change with a recorded, tested exception. Option (a)
requires no new scientific choice and the machinery already exists.

**Nothing else in the package requires changing algorithm behaviour.** In particular the
observer-extension mode suppresses only native termination and not Yuksel's Stage-1 handoff
or Olhoff's S1 trigger, which is the right boundary; and the prohibition on adding common
eigensolves inside Proposed's or Yuksel's optimization loops is stated and correct.

**One implementability risk that is not contamination but is a schedule risk:** the offline
acceptance engine must evaluate `Q(k)` at *every* state, because `Q_ref` is a max over
windows of a window-min and therefore needs the whole trajectory — no bisection is
possible. Estimated from the frozen Olhoff `tEig` scaling
(`t ≈ 1.316e-6 · N_e^1.214` s for a 5-mode solve, `R²` on 9 meshes), the full-budget cost is
**≈ 6.4 h single-threaded for E1-raw across all nine meshes and all three methods**, and
**≈ 38 h for the complete E1/E2/E3 × raw/binary set**. Trajectory storage is ≈ 20 GB
uncompressed. Both are tractable — which *supports* the protocol's claim that the
sensitivity plan needs no extra optimization — but neither appears in the Phase-A estimate
and both should.

## Required final summary

**1. Is R valid as the primary estimand?** Valid in kind, not in its current definition.
Self-referenced maturation is a legitimate primary quantity and the refusal to fabricate an
absolute target is right. But R is horizon-dependent with method-asymmetric horizons (C2),
its margin dominates its own value (M1), and it structurally rewards early plateau and
penalises late improvement (M2). Fix C2 and disclose M1/M2 and it is suitable. It must be
called self-referenced maturation work, never efficiency.

**2. Is A correctly conditional?** Yes. Refusing to instantiate `A` without an independent
target is the right call and should not be weakened. Two corrections: promote the
best-observed benchmark from optional to mandatory (the 6–7 % gap makes it the only
symmetric quality comparison the study can offer), and add a mechanical provenance gate so
no future `Omega_req` can be derived from observed trajectories.

**3. Is T1 topology sanity defensible?** No, as written. The per-component clause is
properly derived from filter resolution and should be kept. The aggregate clause is
undefended, becomes 25× stricter from the coarsest to the finest mesh, and — verified on
14 409 frozen Olhoff states — admits **zero** states at 800x100 and no `P`-length run at
four of nine meshes. Deleting it restores runs of 226 and 298 at 640x80 and 800x100.
`a_res = 5` is arithmetically correct but derives from Olhoff's radius, the smallest of
three (5 / 9 / 21 cells); "r_common" is a misnomer that should be corrected to "strictest
of the three frozen footprints".

**4. Is P = 100 defensible?** As a frozen, disclosed convention whose effects are reported
separately, yes. As a derived value, no — it comes from the Olhoff stabilization policy
(`persistence == 100`, asserted in code) and is applied to methods for which no equivalent
evidence exists, costing Proposed 30–93 % of its native run against Olhoff's 6 %. Keep the
same `P` for all methods — method-specific `P` would create exactly the tunable degree of
freedom the firewall exists to prevent. If `P` is ever re-derived, derive it from measured
false-certification rates on existing trajectories of all three methods, never from desired
counts.

**5. Are `k_enter` and `k_cert` correctly defined and presented?** Defined correctly.
Presented correctly in the tables and figures; the equal-prominence rule is right and
`k_enter` should lead. Two gaps: no rule constrains the abstract or captions, where a single
number will be quoted; and the look-ahead disclosure covers the `P`-window form but not the
much larger whole-budget dependence through `Q^ref`.

**6. Is cross-method method-level iteration comparison meaningful?** Yes, as *design-update
count*, and the protocol is right to refuse false equivalence. It requires the
per-update-cost qualifier to be on the figure rather than in a footnote: at 800x100 the
frozen campaign gives 1.844 s / 0.814 s / 0.489 s per update for Olhoff / Proposed / Yuksel,
a 3.8× spread, with Olhoff's eigensolve alone at 75 % of its outer cost — and Proposed
performs no eigensolve inside its loop at all.

**7. Is Olhoff work accounting honest?** Yes — the most rigorous section of the package.
Every claim I checked is correct: `nInner` is hard-coded to 1 and equals the LP-call count
(`unique(hist.nInner) = [1]`, `sum = 1600`); it is not a HiGHS iteration; the 38-iteration
failed call proves the levels differ; outer updates are the right method-level unit; LP
calls are redundant with outer updates and belong in a footnote; solver-internal iterations
are backend diagnostics and belong in the supplement. No correction needed beyond the
instrumentation-location issue (Q18) and the failure-naming caveat.

**8. Is the timing design fair?** Yes — the strongest part of the package. Warm-up,
single-thread pinning, fixed-horizon replays separated from I/O-heavy discovery runs,
median-of-three with range and MAD, frozen randomised order, fingerprint verification,
full platform capture, and an explicit ban on hardware-independent claims. The one
asymmetry I searched for — Olhoff's `NA` init/finalize — is immaterial in magnitude
(3.47 s against a 2950 s loop at 800x100) and should still be measured because it is cheap.

**9. Are the scaling fits scientifically interpretable?** As empirical scaling over the
tested range, yes — and the spec's prohibition on complexity language is correct and should
be kept. Three limits must be visible: 1.398 decades with five of nine points in the last
half-decade and extreme leverage on the smallest mesh (jackknife moves `p` from +0.145 to
+0.047 on the frozen series); the `k_cert` exponent is partly a `P` artifact (+0.145 →
+0.099); and exponents fitted on different mesh support are not comparable, which the
completed campaign already does (6 / 7 / 9 points) and the new spec does not forbid.

**10. Does the expected-result firewall hold?** In governance and stated intent, yes — the
profile freeze, the blinded acceptance, the censoring discipline, and the register's
self-criticism are all genuine. In the joint direction of its choices, no: six independent
frozen choices all favour the same method, two of them are wrong on the merits, and the
package contains no directional analysis anywhere. A firewall must show its choices are not
jointly directional; this one shows each is individually defensible, which is a weaker and
insufficient claim.

**11. What is the strongest remaining reviewer objection?** After every correction below is
applied, it reduces to: *"You measure how fast each method reaches its own ceiling, and the
ceilings differ by 6–7 % in the competitor's favour."* That is disclosed, intentional,
correctly named, and survivable — provided the quality gap is in the headline table and the
best-observed benchmark is mandatory. Before those corrections, the strongest objection is
Q19 in full, and it would not survive review.
